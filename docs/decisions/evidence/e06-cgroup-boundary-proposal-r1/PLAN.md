## Exact boundary lifecycle

1. **Create and identify.** Create one CID with a fresh run/case label and immutable planned configuration, without auto-removal. Retain the actual create/start/inspect responses. Require exact image, mounts, UID, namespace/isolation, limits and running PID1 identity. Derive the cgroup from that actual PID1 and bind its canonical directory/inode; never construct ownership from a name prefix alone. Reject ancestor paths, aliases, unexpected child cgroups or changed identities. Use exact-CID APIs for lifecycle operations, never numeric HOST PID signals.
2. **Admit before fork.** While PID1 waits without children, verify its HOST/container PID mapping, namespace IDs and membership in the exact pinned cgroup. Only then release START. This is before the first workload fork. Record the actual case start immediately before START; container setup is separately timed preparation, not a fast-start or performance claim.
3. **Register roles.** Preserve actual runner/tracer/role ACKs, source-bound argv/UID/starttime, private pre-fork intents and three sessions. Live identity mismatch still refuses. Running proc samples may aid diagnostics but do not establish closure or authorize unknown PID signaling. Intentional tracer/parent fault signals require exact role-bound validated pidfds acquired and checked against the frozen ACK membership, retaining the original full identity/UID/fdinfo checks. Do not acquire custody of every observed transient, signal provisional descriptors or use bare HOST PIDs. Keep the pinned adapter/provider and explicit close/no-post-reap rules; capability/control evidence must cover this limited role custody.
4. **Freeze READY.** After all declared roles are ready, pause the exact CID and require both actual Docker pause success and the anchored cgroup's `frozen=1` completion. Verify it remains frozen while reading bounded membership, namespace/PID mappings and exact role identities. Require one planned leaf cgroup, no unexpected descendant cgroup and no unknown live member. Known members are PID1, observer runner, tracer and the three ACKed fixture roles, not an argv/count exemption for arbitrary processes. A source-backed role ID is still checked against the actual frozen member. Publish the private READY record while frozen, then unpause. Failure, deadline expiry or uncertain thaw makes the case unqualified.
5. **Observe unchanged capture.** Retain original first-error handling, buffers-suppressed trace, positive/negative sentinel checks, six raw syscall classes, actual stdout/stderr/trace EOF, expected fault, exit and owned tracer reap. Apply fault injection only after the existing exercise barriers. Do not stop clocks during pause or grant another timing allowance.
6. **Reap without concealing leaks.** PID1 remains alive and may nonblockingly reap actual direct/adopted terminated children, recording the real wait results. It must not kill residual workload to obtain a qualifying result. Retain observer/tracer/runner waits separately; do not claim PID1 personally reaped internal children already reaped by their parents. Record actual `waitid` results, including eventual ECHILD, with their scope. ECHILD alone is never whole-graph absence.
7. **Freeze completion.** Before PID1 exits, pause again and require actual frozen completion and the anchored leaf membership to contain **PID1 only**. Pair this live-membership evidence with actual reap/wait records and the supervisor's no-more-forks state. Unknown/residual live tasks or unresolved zombies refuse. `populated=0` is not expected while PID1 is alive; a false zero must not be manufactured. This frozen check prevents PID1 exit from killing leaked descendants and concealing a failed observer cleanup.
8. **Finish and remove.** After accepted completion evidence, thaw and acknowledge PID1's natural exit. Retain actual Docker wait/status/inspect and exact-CID removal/absence. Record a real `populated=0` sample only if obtained; removal of the cgroup is a distinct observation. Any emergency kill/removal, unexpected supervisor exit, lost monitor, missing raw output, residual member or cleanup uncertainty is safety cleanup and remains unqualified. Preserve the first failure and stop later cases; no retry or receipt donation.

The claimed boundary is the exact exclusively administered workload cgroup/PID namespace under the reviewed finite fixture, not a universal defense against a privileged hostile HOST actor. External migration/administration during a frozen observation invalidates it. No performance/CPU attribution follows from these lifecycle checks.
